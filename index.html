How It Works & Honest Science

What "solved" means. A solved experiment is a complete, structured, solver-generated record — question, hypothesis, variables, materials, procedure, a three-trial Universal Matrix, findings, results, conclusion. Solved ≠ physically performed: the computer ran its model of the experiment; nobody went into a lab. Every record carries this as machine-readable fields: status SOLVER-GENERATED · simulation SIMULATED · physically performed NOT-PERFORMED · measured data none.

Deterministic solving. Every record is generated by the fixed solver engine SOLVER-ENGINE-V1 (seed algorithm FNV-1a-32, PRNG mulberry32) from its type and seed — the same inputs always produce the same experiment, on any device, forever. Engine versions are immutable: V1 stays reproducible; new behavior becomes V2. Determinism spec · permanent test vectors · verify any record with ?verify=JAH-EXP-000001.

Honest simulations. Numbers in the measurement tables are computed from built-in physical and statistical models (projectile motion, Hooke’s law, Newton’s cooling, titration curves and the like) to illustrate the method. They are clearly labeled SOLVER SIMULATION and are never presented as laboratory-measured data. Survey-style experiments use seeded illustrative distributions. Every number carries its model, seed, assumptions, and units.

Safety. Every record carries a machine-readable safety classification (SAFE-EDUCATIONAL / ADULT-SUPERVISION / CAUTION / HAZARDOUS / SIMULATION-ONLY / DO-NOT-PERFORM). Experiments involving heat, chemicals, electricity, or projectiles carry explicit safety notes. Young scientists work with adult supervision. "Surprise me" never surfaces hazardous-classified types.

Your measurements welcome. Ran it for real? Each record has an "Add my measurements" section — your numbers are stored on your device and shown next to the simulation for comparison, never overwriting it.

? How to use the Experiment Solver

Describe any experiment in plain words and the solver runs its computer model — a step-by-step simulation with computed numbers. It never performs a physical experiment; every record is labeled SIMULATION.

▶ Run an experiment

  • Type your experiment into the box ("Drop a steel ball from 10 meters and time the fall").
  • Leave the solver on Auto-select, or pick one of the 240 solver types yourself.
  • Press ▶ Run Experiment. Press 🎲 Surprise me for a random experiment — it never picks hazardous-classified types.

⚗ What a run gives you

  • The Universal Matrix: every setting simulated 3 times, step by step.
  • All findings listed, then results and a conclusion.
  • Question, hypothesis, variables (independent / dependent / controlled), materials, procedure, a measurements table (computed, not measured), and a chart.
  • The status line always reads: SOLVER-GENERATED · SIMULATED · physically performed NOT-PERFORMED · measured data none.
  • Every record has its own JAH-EXP number and permanent link (?exp=JAH-EXP-######).

🔍 The archive

  • Search all solved experiments by title — or type JAH-EXP-000123 for an exact record.
  • Filter by discipline, browse the Solver Types A–Z, and press Show more to keep browsing.
  • Every experiment is solved once and stored forever.

🔊 On each record

  • 🔊 Read aloud — a floating bar follows the reading, with Pause and Stop.
  • Copy the record; download it as .txt, .json, or .csv; copy the link; share it.
  • 🔁 Change assumptions & rerun — tweak and run again.
  • ⇄ Compare with previous run — two runs side by side.
  • 📏 Add my measurements — ran it for real? Your numbers are stored on your device, shown next to the simulation, never overwriting it.

⚠ Safety

  • Every record carries a safety classification: SAFE-EDUCATIONAL, ADULT-SUPERVISION, CAUTION, HAZARDOUS, SIMULATION-ONLY, or DO-NOT-PERFORM.
  • The 🤖 Experiment AI answers questions about the record on screen — from the record only, never inventing data — and refuses instructions for dangerous records.

🔍 Verify a record

Open ?verify=JAH-EXP-000001 — the site re-solves the record from its deterministic seed and compares its hash with the published one.
